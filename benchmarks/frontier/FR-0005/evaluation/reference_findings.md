# Finding set F

This set covers conclusions in the main text, Extended Data, and supplementary materials. Coverage is assessed by scientific meaning.

| ID | Author conclusion | Corresponding P* | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| F01 | Except for GAC, maternal and cord-blood IgG against SLO, SpyAD, SpyCEP, and DNAseB do not differ significantly, indicating generally effective placental transfer. Cord-blood GAC levels and fetal:maternal ratios are lower. | P09 | Fig. 2a,b |
| F02 | Maternally derived *S. pyogenes*-specific IgG wanes during the first 11 months after birth in most infants. | P10, P15 | Fig. 2a |
| F03 | Between 6 months and 9–11 months, 22/94 (23%) infants show serological priming consistent with new exposure. The antigen composition of boosting is highly heterogeneous; only two infants show increases against all five antigens. | P15 | Fig. 2a,c |
| F04 | Fetal:maternal IgG transfer ratios at birth do not distinguish infants with subsequent serological evidence of new exposure from those without it. | P14, P15 | Supplementary Fig. 2 |
| F05 | IgG against all five antigens rises rapidly in early childhood and plateaus at approximately 5 years. SLO, SpyAD, and DNAseB IgG declines at older ages. | P20 | Fig. 2d |
| F06 | Within individuals, GAC, SLO, SpyAD, SpyCEP, and DNAseB IgG levels are strongly positively correlated, indicating that responses to conserved antigens accumulate together. | P20 | Supplementary Fig. 3 |
| F07 | Mean IgG against all five antigens increases after culture-confirmed events, with substantial individual heterogeneity. Lower pre-event levels generally correspond to larger absolute post-event increases. | P30 | Fig. 3, Extended Data Fig. 1 |
| F08 | Children aged <2 years show the strongest IgG boosting after culture-confirmed events. Pyoderma, asymptomatic throat/skin carriage, and pharyngitis can produce comparable boosting, indicating that multiple mucosal and skin exposures jointly shape early immunity. | P30 | Fig. 3b, Extended Data Fig. 1b,c |
| F09 | Overall IgG remains stable over 13 months among participants without culture-confirmed events, but those aged <2 years may still show increases missed by culture or waning maternal antibodies. Boosting against conserved antigens therefore has limited serological sensitivity for recent infection. | P30 | Fig. 3c, Discussion |
| F10 | For SLO, SpyAD, and SpyCEP, but not GAC or DNAseB, the relation between IgG and near-term event probability has a plateau at low levels and a decline at high levels. Above the breakpoint, higher IgG corresponds to a lower probability of a culture-confirmed event within 45 days, including after adjustment for age, sex, and household size. | P43 | Fig. 4a–d |
| F11 | Putative 50% protective thresholds from the segmented models are 39,209 RLU/mL for SLO, 65,454 for SpyAD, and 70,643 for SpyCEP. Only a minority of participants exceed these levels at baseline. | P45, P47 | Fig. 4b,e |
| F12 | Having multiple antigens above the strict 50% threshold at the same time point yields only a nonsignificant protective trend. With the more permissive 33% threshold, ≥2 high responses are significantly more protective than one, suggesting that somewhat lower individual responses may provide additive protection. The overall gain from joint-antigen models is limited. | P50 | Extended Data Table 1, Extended Data Fig. 3 |
| F13 | Andersen–Gill recurrent-event analysis provides orthogonal confirmation that high-segment SLO, SpyAD, and SpyCEP IgG is associated with a lower intensity of any culture-confirmed event. The protective signal is clearest for carriage, mainly driven by throat carriage. Estimates for disease, pharyngitis, or skin events are less precise and are not significant for all three antigens. | P56 | Extended Data Tables 2–3 |
| F14 | The life-course profile of anti-M IgG is more heterogeneous than that of conserved-antigen IgG, and cord-blood anti-M levels are lower than maternal levels. Events induce greater boosting against homologous M peptides than against cluster-homologous or unrelated peptides. | P59 | Fig. 5a–c, Supplementary Figs. 4–5 |
| F15 | Higher event-cluster-related anti-M IgG is associated with fewer culture-confirmed events. Matched/cluster-related measures explain the data better than unrelated anti-M, despite limited specificity of some M-peptide assays. | P62, P64 | Fig. 5d, Extended Data Table 4 |
| F16 | Cluster-related anti-M and conserved-antigen IgG are only weakly correlated. Protective associations for SLO, SpyAD, and SpyCEP persist after simultaneous adjustment for anti-M, age, sex, and household size, indicating that anti-M IgG does not fully explain the conserved-antigen signal. A sensitivity analysis restricted to more specific M peptides gives the same direction. | P63, P64 | Fig. 5e–g, Extended Data Fig. 5 |
| F17 | Binding IgG correlates positively with corresponding in vitro functional activity. Samples with SLO, SpyAD, or SpyCEP IgG above the protective threshold show stronger homologous function and more frequent whole-M1 opsonophagocytosis. | P69 | Fig. 6 |
| F18 | Overall, IgG in this high-burden setting is rapidly shaped by waning maternal antibodies, early exposure, and recurrent events. High SLO, SpyAD, and SpyCEP IgG, and to some extent type-related anti-M IgG, are epidemiological correlates of naturally acquired protection in humans. They are not yet validated causal or transferable protective thresholds and require validation in independent cohorts and vaccine trials. | P70 | Abstract, Discussion |
| F19 | Participants aged <2 years show frequent serological priming before their first birthday, but only approximately 2% simultaneously reach the corresponding 50% threshold for every antigen associated with protection. Vaccination at <2 years should be evaluated in future trials and models. Heterogeneity in older children/adults also leaves scope for vaccine-induced increases. | P71, P72 | Discussion |
| F20 | The protective signal is stronger for throat carriage than for skin outcomes, possibly reflecting different relative contributions of humoral immunity and environmental factors across sites. A threshold for any event cannot be directly extrapolated to vaccine protection against pharyngitis or pyoderma. | P73 | Extended Data Table 3, Discussion |
| F21 | Anti-SLO, anti-SpyAD, and anti-SpyCEP may be mechanistically protective antibodies or proxies for unmeasured innate, T/B-cell, or other antigen responses. The absence of an association between GAC and natural protection does not rule out vaccine-induced protective potential for GAC. | P74 | Discussion |
| F22 | Limitations include the absence of microbiological sampling in the mother–infant cohort, events missed by culture and monthly visits, few older participants, selectively tested antibody time points, and insufficient disease-specific statistical power. Protective conclusions are observational associations and cannot establish causality or universal correlates of protection. | P75 | Discussion |
