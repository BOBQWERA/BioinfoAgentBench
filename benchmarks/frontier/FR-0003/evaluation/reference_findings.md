# Reference finding set F

Parentheses identify corresponding P* conclusion nodes. Coverage is assessed by scientific meaning.

## Cohort and overall effects

1. Felzartamab consistently reduces molecular ABMR activity during treatment, from baseline to week 24. All five ABMR classifier/PBT scores show significant negative treatment effects. (C1)
2. During observation after withdrawal, from weeks 24 to 52, all five ABMR scores rebound significantly. No significant net treatment difference remains from baseline to week 52. (C1)
3. Among the 9 felzartamab recipients with molecular ABMR activity at baseline, 9/9 respond at week 24, and 8/9 relapse by week 52. (C3)
4. Suppression is often incomplete in recipients with very high baseline ABMR activity. Patient 4 is the principal exception without molecular relapse; patient 9 has very low baseline molecular activity and does not follow the usual response/relapse trajectory. (C3)
5. None of the five TCMR-related molecular scores shows a significant treatment effect, providing no evidence that felzartamab induces molecular TCMR activity. (C2)
6. Patient 13 has elevated subthreshold TCMR-like transcripts, complicating interpretation of the NK-cell-burden score because T and NK cells share transcripts. (Interpretive conclusion of E6)

## Mechanism and gene expression

7. Highly ranked genes suppressed during treatment are concentrated in IFNγ-inducible and NK-cell-enriched ABMR activity programs. Most reverse direction after withdrawal. (C4, C7)
8. Felzartamab has little effect on endothelial genes associated with ABMR stage, contrasting with its selective suppression of active IFNγ/NK programs. (C4)
9. Findings support the hypothesis that NK-cell suppression is a principal mechanism, rather than an explanation based solely on plasma-cell depletion/DSA reduction. Additional plasma-cell effects cannot be excluded. (C5)
10. Genes increasing at week 24 are more consistent with release from ABMR/IFNγ-mediated suppression of some normal-parenchymal or endothelial expression than with a newly enriched pathogenic program. (C6)
11. From baseline to week 24, 12 ABMR/immune-activity pathways are suppressed. No pathway is FDR-significant from weeks 24 to 52, although genes changing early generally reverse with recurrence. (C7)
12. No individual gene passes genome-wide FDR correction. Single-gene rankings therefore provide exploratory directions, while confirmatory interpretation depends on coordinated gene-set/pathway evidence. (C11)

## Delayed parenchymal-injury effects

13. Injury scores follow a more nearly monotonic trajectory over one year: positive slopes in placebo and negative slopes in felzartamab. Between-arm slope differences for IRITD3 and IRITD5 pass FDR correction; IRRAT30 provides borderline evidence. (C8)
14. Highly ranked differential genes from baseline to week 52 are mostly downregulated injury-induced genes. Genes such as AADACL4 and RERGL, usually suppressed in AKI/ABMR, increase. (C9)
15. All of the top 20 independently defined AKI-marker genes decrease. Enrichment analyses identify suppression of 8 injury-response pathways and 4 immune-related injury-response pathways. (C9)
16. Reduced parenchymal-injury-related expression persists approximately 32 weeks after withdrawal, suggesting that temporary ABMR suppression may yield delayed and durable parenchymal benefits even when ABMR activity returns. (C8, C9, C10)
17. This parenchymal effect may reduce future risk of kidney-function decline/allograft failure, but the study does not directly establish a clinical-outcome benefit. (C10)

## Evidence boundary

18. All efficacy, mechanism and long-term prognostic interpretations come from a small exploratory analysis of 20 recipients with all three visits and require validation in larger trials. (C11)
